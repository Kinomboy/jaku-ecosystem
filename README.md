🌎 JAKU Ecosystem

An independent project in technology, artificial intelligence, and research.

🚧 Under Construction

JAKU is more than an AI assistant. It’s a personal project that has grown into an ecosystem of tools, ideas, and different areas of development.

👋 Hello, I’m the creator of JAKU

My name is Jeisson Andres Polo Motta, and I’m an independent developer based in Colorado, United States.

I started JAKU because I wanted to build something useful for my everyday work.

At first, I wanted an AI assistant that could help me program, understand my projects, solve problems, and remember what we had worked on.

As I continued building it, I started imagining other possibilities.

Why limit it to programming? What if I could use the same foundations for different tools, research projects, experiments, and creative work?

Over time, that idea became JAKU Ecosystem.

I’m not trying to build just another chatbot. I want to create an environment where different tools and projects can work together while still having their own purpose.

⸻

🧩 What’s inside JAKU Ecosystem?

JAKU is made up of several areas. Some already have working components, others are being developed, and some are still ideas I want to explore.

Area	What I’m building
🧠 JAKU Core	The foundation that connects the different parts of JAKU and manages their operations.
💻 JAKU Code	A development workspace for writing code, managing projects, testing, and solving errors.
🤖 JAKU Assistant	An assistant for everyday tasks, information, and useful tools.
⚙️ Models & Agents	Working with different AI models and coordinating specialized tasks.
🗂️ Memory & Projects	Keeping track of decisions, conversations, files, and previous work.
🔗 JAKU-LINK	Connecting JAKU with external tools and local services through controlled interfaces.
🛠️ Engineering Tools	Tools for debugging, auditing, installation, repair, and recovery.
🧪 JAKU Labs	A space for experiments, simulations, testing, and computational research.
🎨 JAKU Media	Exploring creative work involving images, video, and digital content.
🌐 Project Ecosystem	Bringing different projects together without forcing everything into one application.

These are different parts of the same vision. Not every feature is finished or publicly available.

🧪 JAKU Labs — Learning through research

One of the areas I’m especially interested in is JAKU Labs.

I want it to become a place where I can explore scientific questions, test ideas, analyze information, and learn from experiments.

The important part isn’t always finding the answer I expected. Sometimes discovering that an idea doesn’t work is just as valuable.

Research and experimentation areas

Research area	Purpose
🔬 Computational research	Analyze scientific literature, public datasets, and research questions.
🧠 AI model experiments	Explore model capabilities, limitations, and different approaches.
📊 Data analysis	Examine evidence, test hypotheses, and compare results.
🖥️ Hardware experiments	Study how models and tools perform on different computers.
🧪 Simulations	Explore ideas in controlled computational environments.
📚 Reproducibility	Document methods so results can be reviewed and tested again.

🧬 JAKU BIO-LAB MAX

JAKU BIO-LAB MAX is one of the research projects connected to this direction.

I’ve been exploring publicly available scientific research and datasets related to memory and neurodegenerative diseases, including Alzheimer’s disease.

This is independent computational research, not a medical laboratory.

I use AI to help examine information and organize analyses, but that doesn’t mean the results are automatically correct or scientifically confirmed.

There’s still a lot to investigate, verify, and learn.

💻 JAKU Code — Building and solving problems

Programming was one of the main reasons I started JAKU, and it remains an important part of the project.

I want JAKU Code to help with more than generating code in a conversation.

Capability	What I want JAKU to do
📁 Project management	Understand project structures and work with relevant files.
✍️ Programming	Help create, edit, and review code.
🧪 Testing	Run appropriate tests and examine failures.
🔍 Debugging	Investigate errors and identify possible causes.
🔧 Repair	Propose and test corrections within authorized boundaries.
📝 Documentation	Keep useful records of changes and decisions.
🔄 Continuity	Continue previous work without starting over every time.

A lot of my development time has gone into making these processes more reliable.

I’ve learned that generating code is relatively easy compared with making sure it works correctly and doesn’t break something else.

⸻

🛡️ How I want JAKU to work

Building JAKU has taught me that a successful test doesn’t necessarily mean an entire system is reliable.

I’ve dealt with errors, unexpected behavior, incomplete implementations, and changes that worked in one situation but failed in another.

That’s why I want the project to follow a few basic principles.

Principle	What it means to me
✅ Verify the work	Don’t assume something works just because it looks correct.
🔍 Understand errors	Investigate causes instead of repeatedly applying random fixes.
🔐 Keep human control	Important decisions and permissions should remain under human control.
🧠 Preserve useful knowledge	Remember decisions, mistakes, and solutions that can help later.
📚 Respect evidence	Separate observations, hypotheses, and confirmed findings.
🔄 Keep improving	Use failures and feedback to make future work better.
🤝 Share knowledge	Make room for learning and collaboration with other people.

These principles guide the development of JAKU. They are not a claim that every part of the system has already reached these goals.
